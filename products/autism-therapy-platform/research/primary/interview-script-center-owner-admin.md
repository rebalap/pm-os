# Interview Script: Therapy Center Owner / Admin
**Product:** Autism Therapy Platform (India)
**Researcher:** [Name]
**Date:** [Date]
**Session type:** Semi-structured interview
**Duration:** 55–65 minutes
**Target participant:** Center Director, Founder, or Admin Manager — the person responsible for running center operations

---

## Purpose of This Script

This script is for generative, Discovery-stage research. The goal is to understand the lived operational reality of running an autism therapy center in India — not to test a product idea.

**Do not mention the product or any software solution during the session.** Let the participant describe their world before you interpret it.

**Decisions this research will inform:**
- Whether the center director's administrative pain points are severe enough to justify building center operations features (billing, scheduling, inquiry pipeline, dropout tracking)
- Which operations problems are experienced as most acute vs. background noise
- What a center director would need from a tool before they would actually pay for it
- Whether the price sensitivity and willingness-to-pay assumptions are directionally correct

**Hypotheses being tested (from journey-map.md):**
H-01, H-04, H-05, H-06, H-07, H-08, H-09, H-10, H-12, H-14, H-15, H-17, H-18

---

## Researcher Reminders

- You are here to listen, not to validate. Stay curious, stay neutral.
- If the participant jumps to solutions ("what I really wish I had is..."), note it but steer back to behavior: "That's helpful to know — can you tell me about the last time that came up?"
- Use silence. Pause after answers. Participants fill silence with the most revealing details.
- Every opinion is a hypothesis. Every described behavior is evidence.
- Do not say "we're building" or "our product" at any point.

## Section 0 — Warm-Up (5 minutes)

*Goal: Build rapport. Establish the center's context. Calibrate your mental model before asking operational questions.*

**Opening statement (read verbatim or close to it):**

> "Thanks so much for your time today. I want to be clear upfront — I'm not here to sell you anything and there's no product to look at. I'm trying to understand what it's actually like to run a center like yours day to day. Everything you share stays confidential and will only be used to help us understand the space better. There are no right or wrong answers — the more candid you are, the more useful this is for us.
>
> I'll mostly ask questions and listen. Please feel free to go wherever feels relevant to you."

**Questions:**

1. Tell me a bit about your center — how long have you been running it, roughly how many children do you see?

2. What's your role in the day-to-day? Are you mostly on the clinical side, the admin side, or do you wear both hats?

3. Walk me through what a typical Monday looks like for you — from when you arrive to when you leave.

   *Probe if needed:* What are the first things you deal with in the morning? What are you usually still thinking about when you leave?

---

## Section 1 — Inquiry & Enrollment Pipeline (8–10 minutes)

*Goal: Understand how new families find the center and how leads are tracked (or not tracked). Tests H-04.*

*Research question: Do center directors have any visibility into their enrollment pipeline, or does lead tracking live in WhatsApp and memory?*

4. When a new family contacts you for the first time — how does that usually happen?

5. What do you do with that inquiry? Walk me through the steps.

   *Probe:* Where do you write down the family's details? What happens if they don't respond for a few days — how do you remember to follow up?

6. How many new inquiries would you say you get in a month?

   *Probe:* Of those, roughly how many end up enrolling? Do you have a sense of where the others go?

7. Has there ever been a situation where a family reached out, and then you forgot to follow up or lost track of them? What happened?

   *Listen for:* whether they describe a structured system (CRM, register) or informal tracking (WhatsApp scroll, memory). Note the exact tools mentioned.

---

## Section 2 — Intake & Onboarding a New Family (8–10 minutes)

*Goal: Understand what happens when a family comes in for the first time and how documentation is handled. Tests H-06, BP-04.*

*Research question: Is intake structured or ad hoc? What documentation is collected, and how is it stored?*

8. When a family comes in for the first time — the actual visit — what happens? Walk me through it.

   *Probe:* Who is in the room? What documents do you ask for? What do you explain to the family?

9. What do you do with the documents they bring — diagnosis reports, school records, and so on?

   *Probe:* Are they photocopied? Scanned? Do they go into a file? Where does that file live?

10. Is there a consent form that families sign? Can you tell me a bit about what's in it?

    *Listen for:* whether consent covers digital data processing specifically; whether DPDPA or data privacy is on their radar at all. Do not bring up DPDPA yourself — note whether it comes up organically.

11. Is the intake process the same every time, or does it depend on who is handling it that day?

    *Probe:* Have you ever had a situation where a family started therapy and something important wasn't collected or wasn't in their file?

---

## Section 3 — Billing & Fee Collection (10–12 minutes)

*Goal: Understand the actual billing workflow and how center directors experience fee conversations. Tests H-09, H-12.*

*Research question: Is billing manual and WhatsApp-mediated? Do directors delay fee conversations?*

12. How does billing work at your center — how do you charge families, and how often?

    *Probe:* Per session, monthly, in advance? How is the fee amount communicated to families?

13. Walk me through what actually happens at the end of the month from a billing perspective.

    *Probe:* How do you know how many sessions each child had? How is the invoice generated?

14. How do most families pay?

    *Probe:* Cash, UPI, bank transfer? Do you issue receipts? How do you track that payment came in?

15. What happens when a family is late on payment or hasn't paid?

    *Probe:* How do you reach out? What do you say? How many times do you follow up? Be as specific as you can — what does the actual message look like?

16. Is that kind of conversation ever uncomfortable for you?

    *Probe:* Are there families where you've held off on asking because of the situation they're in? What happened?

    *Listen for:* emotional tone, avoidance behavior, whether they describe a backlog of outstanding payments. Note verbatim phrases.

17. At any given moment, do you know which families owe you money and how much?

    *Probe:* How would you find out right now if I asked you?

---

## Section 4 — Attendance, Dropout & Retention (8–10 minutes)

*Goal: Understand whether center directors can see attendance patterns and whether dropout is managed proactively or reactively. Tests H-10, H-18, BP-05.*

*Research question: Does the center have any early warning system for dropout, or is it discovered after the fact?*

18. How do you track attendance — which children came in and which didn't?

    *Probe:* Is that in a register, a spreadsheet, WhatsApp? What does it look like?

19. If a child misses a session, how do you find out?

    *Probe:* Does the therapist tell you? Do you notice it yourself? When in the day do you find out?

20. What do you do when a family misses a session?

    *Probe:* Who reaches out? What do you say? How many times?

21. Have you had families who just... quietly stopped coming? What does that look like from your side?

    *Probe:* When did you realise they weren't coming back? What did you do? In hindsight, were there warning signs?

22. Is there anything you do to try to prevent families from dropping out — before it gets to that point?

    *Probe:* Are there specific families you watch more closely? Why those?

    *Listen for:* whether they describe any proactive monitoring or whether retention is entirely reactive. Note whether they frame dropout as inevitable or as something that can be managed.

---

## Section 5 — Staff Management & Operations Oversight (5–7 minutes)

*Goal: Understand how the director manages clinical staff and what visibility they have into day-to-day clinical quality. Tests H-08, partial H-05.*

*Research question: Does the director have any structured view of what their clinical staff are doing, or does oversight happen through informal proximity?*

23. How many therapists or special educators do you have? How do you stay on top of what they're each doing?

    *Probe:* How do you know if a session went well or badly?

24. How do therapists update you on what happened in a session?

    *Probe:* Is there a session note? Is it written, verbal, WhatsApp? When does it come to you?

25. If you suspected a therapist was struggling — with a child's behavior, or with the program — how would you know? What would that look like?

---

## Section 6 — In-Session Data Collection & Clinical Records (10–12 minutes)

*Goal: Understand what documentation is expected from therapists during and after sessions, how it flows back to supervisors and directors, and what breaks down in that chain. Tests H-01, H-05, H-07, H-14, H-17.*

*Research question: Does the director have visibility into what session data looks like, how reliably it's captured, and what happens to it after the session ends? Is documentation burden experienced as a real operational problem?*

*Note: You are approaching this from the director's perspective — what they expect, receive, and use. Therapist-side experience will be explored in a separate script.*

26. After a therapist finishes a session with a child, what are they supposed to hand over to you or to the clinical lead?

    *Probe:* Is there a specific document, form, or note they fill in? What does it look like?

27. Can you show me — or describe — what one of those session records actually looks like? What's on it?

    *Probe:* Is it a printed sheet, a notebook page, something else? Is it consistent across therapists, or does it vary?

28. Where does that record end up after the session?

    *Probe:* Does it go into a physical file, get photographed, get sent somewhere? How does it reach the supervisor?

29. How quickly does the clinical lead or supervisor typically see the data from a session?

    *Probe:* Same day? End of week? Longer? Is that fast enough, in your view?

30. Have you ever had a situation where session records were missing, illegible, or incomplete? What happened?

    *Probe:* How often does that occur? What do you do when it does?

31. When the supervisor needs to update a child's therapy program, how do they communicate that change to the therapist who delivers the sessions?

    *Probe:* Is it written down somewhere, or is it mostly a verbal conversation? How do you know the therapist understood it correctly?

32. How are progress reports for families put together? Who writes them, and what do they use as source material?

    *Probe:* Do they pull from the session records, or is it written from memory? How long does it typically take to write one?

    *Listen for:* whether reports are assembled from accumulated session data or reconstructed from scratch; whether there's a template; whether the supervisor describes this as burdensome. Note verbatim phrases about time spent.

33. Has a therapist ever sent you a photo of a data sheet or a session note over WhatsApp — just to get it to you faster?

    *Probe if yes:* Is that a regular thing, or a one-off? Who else is in those WhatsApp groups?

    *Listen for:* whether clinical data is routinely transmitted over unencrypted personal messaging. Do not raise privacy concerns yourself — note whether the participant raises them unprompted.

---

## Section 7 — Tools & Workarounds (5 minutes)

*Goal: Inventory what tools are actually in use — without leading toward software. Understand the workaround landscape.*

*Research question: What tools are center directors currently using, and which workflows have no tooling at all?*

34. If I asked you to show me everything you use to run the center day-to-day — what would you show me? Walk me through it.

    *Probe:* WhatsApp groups, spreadsheets, paper registers, a notebook? What's in each one?

35. Is there a task you do every week that you wish you could just... not have to do?

    *Probe:* What makes it tedious? What goes wrong?

36. Have you ever tried any software tools to manage any of this — scheduling apps, billing tools, anything?

    *Probe if yes:* What happened? Are you still using it?
    *Probe if no:* What would need to be different for you to consider trying something?

---

## Section 8 — Wrap-Up (5 minutes)

*Goal: Surface anything not yet covered. End on forward-looking note without pitching.*

37. If you imagine your center running really smoothly — five years from now — what's different about how it works compared to today?

38. Is there anything about running the center that I haven't asked about that you think I should understand?

**Closing statement:**

> "This has been incredibly helpful — thank you for being so open with me. I want to reiterate that everything you've shared is confidential. If it's okay with you, we may reach back out as this research progresses — especially if we want to share what we've learned and get your reaction to it."

## Recruitment Criteria Reminder

This script is designed for participants who match the following profile:

| Criterion | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Role | Center Director, Founder, or Admin Manager — person responsible for billing, enrollment, and staff management |
| Center type | Autism-focused or neuro-developmental therapy center (not general physiotherapy or speech-only) |
| Center size | 5–30 children actively enrolled |
| Location | Metro or Tier-1 city preferred for first round (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Pune) |
| Experience | Running the center for at least 12 months |
| Exclusion | Do not recruit participants currently trialing or using dedicated center management software — that is a separate research segment |

**Target:** 5–8 participants for first round. Aim for mix of founder-clinicians and non-clinical admin managers if possible.
