# Interview Script: Special Educator / Behavior Therapist
**Product:** Autism Therapy Platform (India)
**Researcher:** [Name]
**Date:** [Date]
**Session type:** Semi-structured interview
**Duration:** 45–55 minutes
**Target participant:** Front-line special educator or behavior therapist delivering 1:1 therapy sessions directly with children — the person who holds the data sheet during a live session

---

## Purpose of This Script

This script is for generative, Discovery-stage research. The goal is to understand the lived experience of delivering therapy sessions in an Indian autism center — specifically the in-session data collection workflow and the documentation burden around it.

**Do not mention the product or any software solution during the session.** Let the participant describe their world in their own words first.

**Decisions this research will inform:**
- Whether in-session paper-based data collection is experienced as painful enough to justify a digital alternative
- Whether therapist onboarding burden for any digital tool is a real barrier or a manageable one
- Whether offline-first is a hard product constraint or a nice-to-have, based on actual session room connectivity
- Whether a verbal-only program handover is the real norm and what breaks as a result
- Whether English-only UI is viable at launch for metro-market therapists

**Hypotheses being tested (from journey-map.md):**
H-01, H-02, H-03, H-05, H-08, H-11, H-14, H-16

---

## Researcher Reminders

- This participant is the person doing the clinical work — they are not the buyer, the supervisor, or the admin. Keep questions grounded in their direct experience.
- Therapists may feel judged about paper-based workflows. Never signal that you expect them to be doing something differently. Stay neutral and curious.
- Ask about specific, recent sessions — not hypotheticals. "Tell me about the last time..." surfaces real behavior; "What would you do if..." surfaces idealized self-description.
- The core research question in this session is: **what actually happens in a live session, and what happens after it?** Every section should circle back to observable behavior in that moment.
- Do not raise connectivity, language preference, or tech comfort directly. Let the participant's answers reveal these naturally; then probe.
- Do not say "we're building" or "our product" at any point.

---

## Indian Context Notes for Researcher

Before the session, internalise the following:

- The participant is likely **RCI-licensed** (or training toward it) — not a BCBA. BCBA is a US credential; fewer than ~500 exist in India. Do not assume US-style clinical training.
- Their training likely covered **DTT (Discrete Trial Training)** and possibly **NET (Natural Environment Teaching)**. These are the two dominant methods.
- Paper data collection is likely the **default they were trained on** — not a workaround they fell back on. Treat it as the baseline, not as a failure.
- Their primary device is almost certainly an **Android smartphone** — Redmi, Realme, Samsung mid-range, or similar. Do not assume iOS.
- **WhatsApp** is a professional tool for them, not just personal communication — they likely use it for supervisor updates, scheduling, and parent follow-ups.
- Sessions involve an **active, often dysregulated child** — attention is split. Any description of a data collection step that requires two hands, a screen unlock, or significant focus should be noted.

## Section 0 — Warm-Up (5 minutes)

*Goal: Build rapport. Understand the participant's background, caseload, and daily structure. Calibrate questions to their specific context.*

**Opening statement (read verbatim or close to it):**

> "Thank you for making time for this. I want to be clear — this isn't a performance review, and there are no right or wrong answers. I'm trying to understand what it's actually like to do your job day to day. The more honestly you can describe what really happens — even the parts that feel messy or imperfect — the more useful this conversation is.
>
> I'll mostly ask questions and listen. Feel free to go wherever feels relevant."

**Questions:**

1. Tell me a bit about your role — how long have you been working as a special educator, and what does your caseload look like right now?

   *Probe if needed:* How many children do you see in a day? Are sessions 1:1, or do you sometimes work in groups?

2. What does your training background look like — how did you come into this field?

   *Listen for:* RCI qualification (B.Ed Special Education, D.Ed, PGDRSE), any mention of BCBA, where they trained, whether their training included data collection methods. Note verbatim.

3. Walk me through what a typical day looks like for you — from when you arrive to when you leave.

   *Probe if needed:* How many sessions back to back? Do you have breaks between sessions? What are you doing between children?

---

## Section 1 — Session Preparation (5–7 minutes)

*Goal: Understand how therapists prepare for each session and how the therapy program is communicated to them. Tests H-05, H-08.*

*Research question: Do therapists have a written program reference they can consult before or during a session, or is the program in their head from a verbal briefing?*

4. Before a session starts — let's say five minutes before a child arrives — what do you do?

   *Probe:* Is there anything you refer to? A sheet, a file, something on your phone?

5. How do you know what you're supposed to work on with a specific child in a given session?

   *Probe:* Did someone tell you? Did you read it somewhere? Where is that information kept?

6. When a supervisor or senior therapist updates a child's program — adds a new target, changes a prompt level — how does that change reach you?

   *Probe:* Is it written down? Is it a conversation? When does it happen — before the session, after, on WhatsApp?

7. Has there ever been a situation where you were in a session and you weren't sure exactly what you were supposed to be doing — or you realised you'd been doing something the wrong way for a few sessions? What happened?

   *Listen for:* whether they describe discovering errors days after the fact; whether they have any written reference in the session room or whether the program lives entirely in their head.

---

## Section 2 — In-Session Data Collection (12–15 minutes)

*Goal: Understand in precise detail what happens during a live session — what data is collected, how, and what makes it difficult. This is the most important section of the interview. Tests H-01, H-03, H-14.*

*Research question: Is paper the actual method used? What are the real physical constraints? What gets skipped or compromised under pressure?*

8. During a session, what do you actually write down or record while you're working with the child?

   *Probe:* What does the sheet look like? What are you marking for each trial?

9. Can you show me — or draw / describe — what a data sheet looks like? What's on it?

   *Probe:* Is it printed in advance? Do you fill it in yourself before the session? What happens if you don't have a sheet ready?

10. Walk me through the last session you ran. What were you working on with the child, and how did you record it?

    *This is the most important question in the interview. Let the participant narrate in detail. Do not interrupt. Use silence to let them continue.*

    *Probe after they finish:* Was that a typical session, or was something different about it?

11. What does it feel like physically to record data while you're running a session?

    *Probe:* What are you doing with your hands? Does the child ever make it hard to write at the same time?

    *Listen for:* one-handed constraint, pen drops, data sheet on floor/table/clipboard, child grabbing materials, therapist breaking eye contact. Note specific physical descriptions verbatim.

12. Are there moments in a session where you don't write something down, even though you meant to? What causes that?

    *Probe:* Does it happen because the child is dysregulated? Because you're rushed? Because the format is hard to fill in quickly?

    *Listen for:* whether data gaps are common and normalized, or rare. Do not suggest either.

13. What do you do when a child has a behavioral episode during a session — something like a tantrum, self-injury, or aggression? How do you record that?

    *Probe:* Do you write it down during the session or after? What do you note — just that it happened, or what triggered it?

14. At the end of a session, what does your data sheet look like? How complete is it, honestly?

    *Probe:* Are there gaps? Things you meant to note that you didn't? Is that normal?

15. After the session ends, what happens to that data sheet?

    *Probe:* Does it go to your supervisor? Into a file? Does anyone ever take a photo of it? What do you do if the supervisor isn't there?

    *Listen for:* whether WhatsApp photo is described. If so, probe: "Is that something you do regularly? Who gets that photo?"

---

## Section 3 — Post-Session Documentation (8–10 minutes)

*Goal: Understand what written documentation exists beyond the data sheet and when it is created. Tests H-07 (partial), H-17 (partial).*

*Research question: Is there a session note? When is it written? How much time does post-session documentation take, and is it done during work hours?*

16. After a session ends, is there anything you write up — a session note, a summary of what happened?

    *Probe:* What does it say? How long does it take? When do you write it — immediately, or later?

17. Is there anything you're supposed to document that you often end up doing later than you should — or sometimes skip?

    *Probe:* What makes it hard to keep up with?

18. On a typical day, roughly how long after your last session do you leave the center?

    *Probe:* Is that time spent with children, or doing paperwork?

    *Listen for:* whether documentation extends beyond clinical hours; whether they describe staying late or taking work home. Note verbatim.

19. Has your supervisor ever asked you about documentation that wasn't there, or wasn't clear enough? What did they say?

---

## Section 4 — Supervisor Communication & Program Confidence (5–7 minutes)

*Goal: Understand the quality of the feedback loop between therapist and supervisor. Tests H-05, H-08.*

*Research question: Does the therapist feel confident they understand the current program? How long does it take for supervisor feedback to reach them after a session?*

20. After your supervisor reviews a child's data, how do you find out what they thought — or what changes they want to make?

    *Probe:* How quickly does that usually happen after the sessions were run?

21. Have you ever continued running a target that had already been mastered — or run it at the wrong prompt level — because you didn't know it had been updated? How did you find out?

22. If you had a question right now about a specific child's program — what they should be working on this week, or what prompt level to use — how would you find the answer?

    *Probe:* Would you message your supervisor on WhatsApp? Look at a document? Ask a colleague?

---

## Section 5 — Tools, Devices & Connectivity (7–8 minutes)

*Goal: Understand the device landscape, tech comfort, connectivity experience, and language preferences — without leading the participant toward any conclusion. Tests H-02, H-03, H-11, H-16.*

*Research question: What phone do they use? What apps do they use for work? Have they ever used clinical software? What language do they prefer for any work-related tool?*

23. What phone do you use for work? Is it your personal phone, or does the center provide one?

    *Probe:* What do you mainly use it for during the workday?

24. Are there any apps — other than WhatsApp — that you use for work at the center?

    *Probe:* Anything for scheduling, notes, files, progress tracking? Have you ever used any software specifically for therapy documentation?

    *If they mention any tool, probe:* What do you use it for? Do you still use it? What made you start or stop?

25. The room where you run most of your sessions — how is the mobile signal in there?

    *Probe:* Have you ever had a moment where you needed to look something up on your phone and the data wasn't working?

    *Listen for:* whether connectivity is a known problem or never comes up. Do not frame this as a problem yourself.

26. At your center, does the clinical work — forms, notes, any instructions from your supervisor — happen in English, or in another language?

    *Probe:* Is that comfortable for you? If you were using an app for your work, would you prefer it to be in English, or in Hindi or your regional language?

    *Listen for:* any hesitation or self-editing. Note verbatim. Do not assume English is fine.

27. Has anyone at your center ever introduced a new tool or app that you were asked to start using? What was that experience like?

    *Probe:* Did you find it easy to pick up? What made it hard or easy? Are you still using it?

    *Listen for:* whether prior digital tool adoption has been positive or negative; what specific barriers they describe.

---

## Section 6 — Wrap-Up (5 minutes)

*Goal: Surface anything not yet covered. Give the participant space to say what matters most to them.*

28. If there's one part of your job that takes up time or energy in a way that feels unnecessary or frustrating — what would it be?

    *Probe:* What would a better version of that look like?

29. Is there anything about what you actually do in sessions — or after them — that you think most people outside this field don't understand?

30. Is there anything I haven't asked about that you think is important for me to understand?

**Closing statement:**

> "This has been really valuable — thank you for walking me through your actual day. Everything you've shared is confidential. We may reach out again as this research continues, especially if we want your reaction to what we've been learning more broadly. Would that be okay?"

## Recruitment Criteria Reminder

This script is designed for participants who match the following profile:

| Criterion | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Role | Special Educator, Behavior Therapist, or ABA Therapist delivering direct 1:1 therapy sessions with children |
| Certification | RCI-licensed or in training (B.Ed Special Education, D.Ed, PGDRSE, or equivalent). BCBA acceptable but not required — do not screen for it |
| Center type | Autism-specific or neuro-developmental therapy center |
| Seniority | Front-line therapist — not primarily supervisory. Should be personally running sessions with children most days |
| Experience | At least 6 months in current center |
| Location | Metro or Tier-1 city preferred for first round |
| Exclusion | Do not recruit center directors or clinical supervisors for this script — use the owner/admin script for those roles |

**Target:** 8–10 participants for first round. Aim for a mix of experienced therapists (3+ years) and newer practitioners (6–18 months) — onboarding burden and tech comfort may differ significantly between these groups.

**Pairing note:** Where possible, recruit therapist-supervisor pairs from the same center so that H-05 (program handover) can be triangulated from both sides.
