# Interview Script: In-Center Generative Research
## Special Educator / Therapist and Center Director / Owner Variants

**Product:** Autism Therapy Platform (India)
**Stage:** Discovery — Generative research
**Session length:** 60–75 minutes
**Variants:** This script covers two participant types. Questions marked [THERAPIST] are for special educators and behavior therapists only. Questions marked [DIRECTOR] are for center directors and owners only. Unmarked questions are shared.
**Research goal:** Understand the world as it is — workflows, pain, workarounds, and the texture of daily clinical and administrative life in Indian autism therapy centers. We are not testing a product or concept.

**Last updated:** 2026-04-14
**Prepared by:** Research Analyst

---

## Session Setup Checklist

Complete before the participant arrives or the video call begins.

- [ ] Consent form signed and copy given to participant (paper or digital)
- [ ] Recording consent confirmed verbally at session start — state what recording will be used for, who will hear it, and that they can stop at any time
- [ ] Note-taker briefed: capture direct quotes verbatim, note non-verbal reactions, do not paraphrase
- [ ] Observer (if present): briefed to remain silent; observer-specific debrief scheduled after session
- [ ] Session timer visible to interviewer; section time budgets printed on this document
- [ ] Participant profile confirmed in advance: role, years of experience, center size (approximate), city
- [ ] Stimulus materials: none — this is a pure generative interview. No mockups, no demos.
- [ ] Confirm: phone or laptop on silent; door sign or Zoom status set to "in session"
- [ ] Open questions document for live note capture
- [ ] Debrief form (Section 6) printed or open in a separate tab

## Preamble to Read Aloud
*(Read this verbatim or close to it. Do not improvise the consent elements.)*

"Thank you for making time today. I want to be clear about what this conversation is and isn't. We are researchers, not salespeople. We are trying to understand how autism therapy centers in India actually work day-to-day — the real workflows, what goes smoothly, what is frustrating, what gets improvised. There are no right or wrong answers. We are not testing you.

We will be asking about your daily work in some detail. Please feel free to say 'I don't know,' 'that doesn't apply to us,' or 'I'd rather not answer that.' Nothing you say will be shared with your employer, parents, or any regulatory body. We will quote anonymized observations in internal research documents — if you say something we want to quote directly, we will check with you first.

With your permission, I'd like to record this session so I can focus on listening rather than note-taking. The recording stays within our research team and is deleted after synthesis. Are you comfortable with that?

Any questions before we begin?"

## Section 1 — Warm-Up: Role, Day, and Context
**Time budget: 10 minutes**
**Purpose:** Build rapport. Establish the participant's role, experience, and the size and shape of their center — without asking leading questions. Ground the conversation before we get into specifics.

*Interviewer note: Do NOT mention software, apps, or technology in this section. Let the participant establish their own frame for their work. If they bring up technology, note it but redirect back to the workflow.*

---

**Q1.** Can you start by telling me a bit about your role and how long you've been doing this work?

> - What does your title actually mean in practice — what does a typical week look like for you?
> - How did you come into this field? Did you train formally, or did you arrive here through a different path?

*Note: Listen for: RCI licensure mentioned or not, years in practice, how they describe their own role vs. formal title. These shape how to frame later questions.*

---

**Q2.** Tell me about the center you work at — or run. What kind of place is it?

> - How many staff members? How many children on your caseload, roughly?
> - Is it a dedicated autism center, or does it serve other children with different needs too?
> - Where is it located — and what does the neighborhood or catchment area mean for the families you serve?

*Note: This establishes center scale (5–20 staff is typical). Probe gently on center size — this affects whether workflows are founder-managed or delegated. Do not lead with a number range.*

---

**Q3.** Walk me through what yesterday actually looked like — from when you arrived until when you left.

> - What were you doing first thing?
> - Were there any moments in the day where you had to stop what you were doing and deal with something unexpected?
> - What was the last thing you did before you left?

*Note: This is a "day in the life" anchor question. It often surfaces the most honest pain without the participant feeling interrogated. Let them ramble. Follow threads. Capture specific activities and moments — not generalizations.*

---

## Section 2 — In-Session Work and Data Collection
**Time budget: 15 minutes**
**Purpose:** Understand what actually happens during a therapy session — what data is recorded, by whom, in what format, with what friction. We want behavioral specifics, not stated preferences.

*Interviewer note: The goal here is to understand current practice — not to evaluate it or propose alternatives. If a participant says "we should really be doing X," redirect: "What do you actually do today?"*

---

**Q4. [THERAPIST]** Take me through what a session actually looks like when you're working with a child. Walk me through it like I've never seen one.

> - How do you know what you're working on with that child that day?
> - At what point — if at all — are you writing something down or noting something during the session?
> - What are you writing on? Paper, a phone, something else?
> - How much of your attention is on the child vs. on recording?

*Note: This is deliberately open. Do NOT mention specific data types (DTT, frequency, ABC) unless the participant introduces these terms themselves. Let the participant describe their own practice before reflecting back any technical vocabulary.*

---

**Q5. [THERAPIST]** When you do record data during a session, what exactly are you recording?

> - Can you show me or describe the sheet/format you use? What does it look like?
> - Are there things you're supposed to record that you don't, in practice? Why not?
> - Does what you record change depending on which child you're working with?

*(KEY ASSUMPTION CHECK: We assume special educators record data during live sessions in some structured form. This question surfaces whether recording happens at all, how structured it is, and what gets skipped.)*

*Note: The gap between "what they should record" and "what they actually record" is the most important data point in this section. Probe hard on this without judgment.*

---

**Q6. [THERAPIST]** What happens to the notes or data after the session is over?

> - Who sees it, and when?
> - Does anyone review it or use it to make decisions about the child's program?
> - Have there been times when something got missed or lost? What happened?

*Note: This surfaces the supervisor feedback loop — or absence of it. Note whether data is ever actually used for clinical decisions, or whether it exists primarily as a paper record no one reviews.*

---

**Q7. [DIRECTOR]** When you want to understand how a child is progressing, how do you get that information?

> - What does a clinical supervisor or senior therapist actually bring you, and how often?
> - When there's a concern about a child's progress — something isn't working — how does that surface? Who raises it? Through what channel?
> - How do you know what's happening in sessions you're not in the room for?

*Note: This surfaces whether directors have real-time visibility or rely on escalation. If they say "I trust my team," probe: "Walk me through a recent time you had a concern about a specific child."*

---

**Q8.** Tell me about the space where sessions happen — physically.

> - Is there a table, a chair, a desk? What's within arm's reach during a session?
> - What's the phone or internet situation in your session rooms? Is the signal reliable?
> - Do you ever have sessions where connectivity is a problem?

*(KEY ASSUMPTION CHECK: We assume connectivity is intermittent enough in a meaningful proportion of Indian therapy centers to make offline capability a hard requirement. This question surfaces the real connectivity situation without leading the participant toward an expected answer.)*

*Note: Do not mention offline or connectivity as a product concept. Frame as understanding the physical environment. The answer will reveal whether connectivity is a daily concern, an occasional nuisance, or a non-issue.*

---

## Section 3 — Intake and Enrollment
**Time budget: 10 minutes**
**Purpose:** Understand what the enrollment process actually looks like — from first contact to first session — and where administrative and clinical friction accumulates.

*Interviewer note: In this section, the center director variant carries more weight. If speaking with a therapist, focus on their direct experience of receiving a new child onto their caseload, rather than the administrative enrollment side. Adapt accordingly.*

---

**Q9. [DIRECTOR]** When a family first reaches out to your center, walk me through exactly what happens next.

> - Where does that first contact come in — phone, WhatsApp, in person, something else?
> - Who handles it, and what do they say or send to the family?
> - What happens between that first contact and the child actually starting sessions?

*Note: Map the steps in sequence. Note where the participant mentions delays, handoffs, follow-ups, and drop-off. Ask about timeline explicitly if they don't offer it.*

---

**Q10. [DIRECTOR]** From first inquiry to a child's first therapy session — roughly how long does that take? And what eats the time?

> - Are there steps in that process where families wait for longer than you'd like?
> - Have you had families who made initial contact but never actually enrolled? What happened to them?
> - Do you track how many inquiries turn into enrollments?

*(KEY ASSUMPTION CHECK: We assume centers do not systematically track their enrollment pipeline — that inquiry-to-enrollment conversion is unmeasured. This question surfaces whether any tracking exists, and what happens to families who don't convert.)*

*Note: Note whether the participant has any number in mind for inquiry-to-enrollment conversion. Most will not. This is informative. Do not offer a benchmark.*

---

**Q11. [DIRECTOR]** What documents or information do you collect from a family before or during intake?

> - What do they bring in? What do you ask for?
> - Is there anything you're supposed to collect that families often don't have, or can't provide?
> - What happens to those documents after intake — where do they live?

*Note: Probe on digital vs. paper, and whether documents are filed in a way that's findable later. Note UDID, diagnosis reports, prior therapy records specifically — but only if participant raises them first.*

---

**Q12. [DIRECTOR]** After the initial assessment — whatever form that takes at your center — how do you communicate the therapy plan to the family?

> - What do they actually receive? Is it written down, explained verbally, sent over WhatsApp?
> - How much do parents typically understand about what will happen in sessions?
> - Have there been situations where parents expected something different from what was planned?

*Note: This surfaces the assessment-to-program communication gap. Note whether anything is documented for the family in a comprehensible format, or whether the communication is primarily verbal.*

---

**Q13. [THERAPIST]** When a new child joins your caseload, how do you learn what to work on with them?

> - What information do you receive — from a supervisor, a file, verbally?
> - Is there a document or plan you work from, or do you build it yourself over the first few sessions?
> - What's missing from what you receive that you wish you had?

*Note: This surfaces how assessment findings travel from the evaluating clinician to the treating therapist. Note any handoff failures or information gaps.*

---

## Section 4 — Treatment Planning, Reporting, Billing, and Follow-Up
**Time budget: 15 minutes**
**Purpose:** Understand how documentation-heavy tasks — treatment plan authorship, progress reports, billing, and missed session follow-up — are handled, by whom, and where they break down.

*Interviewer note: This section has the highest density of sensitive topics (billing, fee conversations, dropout). Maintain a neutral, curious tone. If a participant deflects ("billing is fine"), probe gently with specifics: "Can you walk me through the last time you had a conversation with a family about fees?"*

---

**Q14. [DIRECTOR] / [THERAPIST — if senior/supervisor role]** How do treatment plans get created or updated at your center?

> - Who writes the plan? How long does it take?
> - How often are plans reviewed or updated? What triggers an update?
> - Do you have a standard format or template, or does it vary by therapist?

*Note: The intent is to surface whether treatment plan authorship is centralized or distributed, and how burdensome it is in practice. Note whether any number of hours per week is mentioned voluntarily.*

---

**Q15. [DIRECTOR] / [THERAPIST — if senior/supervisor role]** Progress reports for families — how do those get done?

> - Who writes them, and how often do they go out?
> - Walk me through how one actually gets produced — what goes into it, where the information comes from, how long it takes.
> - What time of day do you usually find yourself doing this work?

*Note: "What time of day" is a deliberate probe for after-hours documentation — a proxy for uncompensated time and burnout pressure. If they say evenings or weekends, do not express surprise — probe further: "Is that typical?" The question is designed to surface this without leading with "do you work late?"*

---

**Q16. [DIRECTOR]** How do you collect fees from families?

> - What does that conversation typically look like — is it a WhatsApp message, a phone call, a conversation at the center?
> - If a family is behind on fees, how do you handle it? Who has that conversation?
> - How often does that happen — families getting behind?

*Note: This is a high-sensitivity question. Normalize it with a neutral framing. If the director says "it doesn't really happen," probe: "Walk me through how a family becomes a client financially — from the first session to a month in." Most will reveal the informal reality through that reconstruction.*

---

**Q17. [DIRECTOR]** When a child misses a session, what happens on your end?

> - Who notices first — is there a system, or does someone just see an empty room?
> - What do you do next? Who reaches out, and through what channel?
> - Have you had families who just... stopped coming? What did that look like?

*Note: This surfaces whether missed session follow-up is a protocol or an improvised act. Note whether the participant mentions any pattern of dropout — and whether they think of it as a process failure or an outcome they have no control over.*

---

**Q18. [DIRECTOR]** Over the past year, do you have a sense of how many families have disengaged or dropped out?

> - Is that something you track?
> - When you think of a family who left, what usually seemed to be the reason?
> - Is there a point in the therapy journey where you see it happen more often?

*Note: Most directors will not have a number. That is itself a finding. Do not volunteer any statistics. Note whether the word "dropout" resonates or whether the participant uses a different frame ("they moved," "they felt ready," "the child graduated").*

---

## Section 5 — Strain, Workarounds, and the Bigger Picture
**Time budget: 10 minutes**
**Purpose:** Surface the texture of daily frustration, the workarounds people have invented, and the moments when the current system fails completely. This section often yields the richest problem signal.

*Interviewer note: This is an open-ended section. Let the participant lead. Your job is to follow threads and ask "tell me more about that" repeatedly. Resist the urge to solve or validate — just listen.*

---

**Q19.** What parts of your week feel the most chaotic or draining — things that eat time you wish you had for something else?

> - Can you give me a recent specific example?
> - What do you do when that happens?

*Note: Do not suggest categories. Let them name the pain themselves. Note whether administrative work, clinical documentation, parent communication, or staff coordination comes up unprompted.*

---

**Q20.** Is there something you do at work that you've figured out your own way of doing — a workaround, a shortcut, something that isn't "official" but it works for you?

> - When did you start doing it that way?
> - Has anyone else in the center adopted it?

*Note: Workarounds are windows into unmet needs. The best ones reveal: (a) that a real problem exists, (b) that someone was motivated enough to solve it, and (c) the shape of the solution they reached for. Take detailed notes.*

---

**Q21.** Is there anything you think your center does really well — something that works and that you're proud of?

*Note: This question serves two purposes: it gives the participant a moment to reflect positively (rapport), and it surfaces what "good" looks like in their context — which benchmarks later evaluative research.*

---

**Q22.** If you had to describe the single biggest difference between a center that runs well and one that doesn't — what would it be?

> - Have you seen that difference in practice — a place that did it better or worse?

*Note: This is a peer-benchmarking question. Directors especially will often reveal detailed knowledge of how other centers operate. Follow any comparison they make.*

---

**Q23. [DIRECTOR]** I want to ask about something specific — data privacy and consent when collecting information from families, especially for children. How do you think about that at your center?

> - Do you have any formal consent process for the data you store about children and families?
> - Have you heard of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act — DPDPA 2023?
> - Is that something you've discussed internally, or consulted anyone about?

*Note: This tests awareness of DPDPA 2023 without leading on what the correct answer is. Most directors will be unaware or have only passing familiarity. Note their reaction to the question — is it concern, dismissal, curiosity? Probe if they are aware: "What have you done about it?" Do not explain the law unless asked.*

---

## Section 6 — Close
**Time budget: 10 minutes**
**Purpose:** Give the participant space to add anything they want on their own terms, ask us anything, and end on a question that surfaces aspirational pain — the place where they feel most stuck.

*Interviewer note: The magic wand question must come last, after everything else has been asked. It is the only place in the interview where we allow the participant to think in terms of solutions. At all other points, redirect if they start proposing solutions: "That's interesting — what makes you want that? Tell me more about the problem it would solve."*

---

**Q24.** Is there anything about how your center works that we haven't talked about — something you think we should understand?

*Note: Hold silence here. This question often yields the most important data point of the session. Don't rush past the pause.*

---

**Q25.** Are there any questions you have for us?

*Note: Answer honestly about the research purpose. If asked about what we're building, say: "We're still in the early phase of understanding the problem — we haven't decided what to build yet. That's exactly why this conversation is valuable."*

---

**Q26 — Close.** Last question: if you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about how your center runs, what would it be?

> - What would be different, specifically?
> - Why that, above everything else?

*Note: This is the only aspirational or solution-adjacent question in the session and must be placed last. The goal is not to hear feature requests — it is to understand what pain is most salient and most urgent to this participant. A director who says "I wish I could see at a glance which families are behind on fees" is revealing something about the pain of financial opacity, not requesting a dashboard feature.*

---

**Close script:**

"Thank you so much — this has been genuinely useful. The kind of detail you shared is exactly what we can't find from reading reports or talking to experts. We may follow up with a few clarifying questions over WhatsApp or email if that's all right. Is that okay?

We'll let you know how the research develops. We expect to share back a summary of what we learn across all our conversations — we'll reach out when that's ready."

## Appendix A — Participant Recruitment Criteria Reference

**For the therapist variant:**
- RCI-licensed special educator or behavior therapist
- At least 1 year of direct ABA or early intervention therapy experience
- Currently working at an autism therapy center in India (not a school or general disability center)
- Metro or Tier-1 city preferred for first wave (Delhi, Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Chennai, Mumbai, Pune)
- Include at least 1 participant from a center of under 8 staff and 1 from a center of 12 or more

**For the director variant:**
- Center director, founder, or owner of an autism therapy center
- Operational responsibility for the center (not a titular head)
- Center currently active — minimum 10 enrolled children
- At least 2 years running the center
- At least 1 participant should also be a practicing clinician (founder-clinician profile is common)

**Exclusion criteria (both variants):**
- Participants who work at centers already using purpose-built ABA data collection software (rare in India, but verify at recruitment)
- Participants whose center primarily serves non-autism diagnoses
- Participants recruited through any connection to the product team or founding organization

**Recruitment method:**
- RCI-affiliated training programs and autism advocacy networks (Action For Autism, Autism Society India)
- WhatsApp groups for special educators and center directors in target cities (approach via mutual connection, not cold message)
- Snowball from initial participants — therapists often know peer center directors
- Do not use LinkedIn cold outreach as primary channel — clinical staff are unlikely to respond

**Number of sessions:**
- Target 6–10 participants across both variants
- Minimum 3 therapists and 3 directors before beginning synthesis
- Stop adding participants when new sessions produce no new themes (thematic saturation)

| 1 — Warm-up | Role, day, center context | 10 min |

| 3 — Intake and enrollment | Inquiry to first session | 10 min |
| 4 — Treatment plans, billing, follow-up | Documentation, fees, dropout | 15 min |
| 5 — Strain and workarounds | Friction, improvisation, peer benchmark | 10 min |
| 6 — Close | Open addition, magic wand | 10 min |
| **Total** | | **75 min** |

*If a section runs long, compress Sections 3 or 5 first. Never compress the close — Q26 (magic wand) and the silence after Q24 frequently produce the most usable data of the session.*
