- **🇮🇳 DPDPA 2023 — Children's health data rules active:** Rule 10 of the DPDP Rules 2025 requires verifiable parental/guardian consent (OTP, ID verification, or secure portal) before any processing of a child's personal data. The DPDP Rules separately reference the National Trust for Welfare of Persons with Autism Act — autism therapy centers are explicitly within scope. The DPDPA compliance deadline is **May 2027**, with a 12-month active preparation window underway across Indian industries. Prohibited processing activities include behavioral monitoring and tracking of children (with healthcare exemptions available for legitimate clinical purposes). — *ATP implication: ATP processes health data of minors — the highest-sensitivity category under DPDPA. Verifiable parental consent flows and data residency within India are non-negotiable architecture constraints, not post-launch features. The healthcare exemption exists but requires demonstrable legitimate purpose documentation.* ([dpdpa.com compliance roadmap](https://dpdpa.com/blogs/dpdpa_compliance_deadline_2027_12_month_action_plan.html), [assurtiv.com DPDP child rules](https://assurtiv.com/child-data-protection-rules-2025-under-dpdp-act/), [consent.in parental consent](https://www.consent.in/blog/child-consent))

## 💡 Strategic Implication

Theralytics' consolidation as US market leader confirms that the winning pattern is session data flowing directly into billing and reporting without manual handoff — while Raven Health's data-collection-only positioning demonstrates a viable narrow entry strategy exists. ATP's data-collection-first MVP mirrors Raven, but must have a credible integration roadmap to avoid being trapped at Tier 1. The more urgent constraint is DPDPA: with the May 2027 compliance deadline 13 months away and explicit rules now published for children's health data, ATP's consent architecture and data residency decisions need to be locked in before any build begins — not deferred to a compliance sprint.
