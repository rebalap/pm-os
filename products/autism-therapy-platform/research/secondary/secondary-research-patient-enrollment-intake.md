# Secondary Research Synthesis: Patient Enrollment & Intake in Autism Therapy Centers (Web Search Update)

**Product:** Autism Therapy Platform (India)
**Date:** 2026-04-13
**Stage:** Discovery
**Methods used:** Web search, desk research (live searches conducted April 2026)

---

## Key Themes

1. There is no standardized intake protocol or critical pathway for autism therapy enrollment in India — the process is fragmented, medically-mediated, and entirely paper-based.
2. Families arriving at therapy centers have already navigated a long, exhausting diagnostic journey — intake is a high-stakes trust moment, not just an administrative task.
3. Indian regulatory frameworks (RPWD Act 2016, DPDPA 2023, UDID scheme) create specific documentation obligations at enrollment that are largely unmet by current informal workflows.
4. No purpose-built intake tool exists for Indian autism therapy centers; US tools assume HIPAA, insurance, and BCBA role structures that don't apply.
5. Dropout begins at enrollment — families who experience a disorganized or unclear intake are more likely to disengage early.
6. DPDPA 2023 creates real compliance risk for centers storing health data of minors digitally without verifiable parental consent.

---

## Findings by Topic

### 1. What Intake Involves and How It's Managed Today

**The structural problem — no standardized pathway:**

"There is no standardized protocol or critical pathway for screening, identification, and referral in India, with the process being mediated by a purely medical model where children may or may not get access to therapy despite services being available in the community." Medical professionals and therapy services lack coordination. Source: [PMC — Autism community priorities in India](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10771024/)

"Parents must look for medical professionals and therapy by engaging with other parents, and there is a lack of formal structure to the holistic model of service from the red flags stage to therapy and mainstreaming." Source: [PMC — Autism community priorities in India](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10771024/)

**What a typical intake process involves:**

- Initial inquiry (typically via WhatsApp or phone call)
- Developmental history intake interview with parents
- Collection of prior documentation: diagnosis reports, school records, prior therapy reports
- UDID certificate (where applicable)
- Standardized assessments to establish baseline: ISAA, CARS, Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales
- Program planning by senior therapist based on assessment results
- Administrative setup: fee agreement, consent forms, session scheduling, parent orientation

[ASSUMPTION] Most of this process is managed through paper forms, WhatsApp, and physical files — not any structured digital system.

**UDID documentation at enrollment:**

The UDID card is a government-issued disability ID under the RPWD Act 2016. Therapy centers are frequently asked by parents to provide supporting documentation for UDID applications. "School/therapy reports (for children: special education reports, speech therapy reports) are useful forms of supporting documentation to demonstrate the functional impact." Source: [India Autism Center — UDID guide](https://indiaautismcenter.org/blog/how-to-get-an-autism-disability-certificate-in-india-apply-for-udid/)

[ASSUMPTION] Centers that serve families pursuing UDID face periodic documentation requests that are currently managed ad hoc — a recurring administrative overhead with no structured workflow.

**Access gaps that affect enrollment timing:**

"Parents often have to wait for years or travel across the country to get a confirmed diagnosis of autism." Trained professionals are concentrated in urban cities. Source: [PMC — Autism in India](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10438407/)

"Interview respondents expressed dissatisfaction with intervention services, describing them as unavailable and inaccessible." Source: [PMC — Autism community priorities](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10771024/)

### 2. Existing Tools and Their Relevance to Indian Centers

**US-origin practice management tools:**

Tools like SimplePractice, TherapyNotes, Jane App, and Cliniko are designed for insurance-reimbursed Western therapy markets. Their intake features assume HIPAA compliance frameworks, insurance pre-authorization, and BCBA/RBT credentialing structures — none of which apply in India. Pricing at $30–80+/month is structurally incompatible with Indian center economics.

[ASSUMPTION] None of these tools are in meaningful use in Indian autism therapy centers.

**Indian healthcare software:**

Practo and similar platforms support general outpatient appointment scheduling and basic patient registration. They do not include autism-specific assessment tools (ISAA, CARS, Vineland), therapy program structure, behavioral data collection, or RPWD/UDID documentation support.

**TherapEZ (India):**

An Indian platform offering scheduling, data management, and parent communication for therapy centers. Does not specifically address autism intake workflows, assessment-to-program handoff, or UDID documentation. Source: [TherapEZ](https://therapez.in/2024/11/14/autism-trends-2025/)

**Gap:** No purpose-built, India-specific intake tool for autism therapy centers was found.

### 3. Indian Regulatory and Operational Context

**RPWD Act 2016:**

Autism spectrum disorder is one of 21 specified disabilities under the RPWD Act 2016. Persons with a disability of at least 40% are eligible for UDID and associated benefits. The Act mandates documentation of individualized programs for children with disabilities. Sources: [Disability Rights India](https://www.disabilityrightsindia.com/p/the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities_27.html); [India Code — RPWD Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2155?sam_handle=123456789/1362)

**DPDPA 2023:**

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 applies to digital processing of personal data including health data. Key obligations:

- **Minors' data requires verifiable parental/guardian consent.** "For individuals below 18 years of age, consent will be provided by the parent or the legal guardian." Source: [DPDPA.com — Section 9](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section9.html)
- A published PMC analysis examined DPDPA implications for mental healthcare, noting health data misuse carries significant harm risk. Source: [PMC — DPDPA and Mental Healthcare](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12423081/)
- A healthcare exemption exists for essential health/education purposes, but consent collection remains required as the primary mechanism

[ASSUMPTION] Most Indian autism therapy centers are not currently DPDPA-compliant — they use informal tools without formalized consent processes for digital data.

**UDID enrollment support as an intake function:**

The UDID process involves formal application, medical board assessment, and issuance within three months. Therapy centers assist families — requiring formal therapy reports, assessment documentation, and developmental history records. Source: [India Autism Center — UDID guide](https://indiaautismcenter.org/blog/how-to-get-an-autism-disability-certificate-in-india-apply-for-udid/)

### 4. Parent Engagement at Enrollment

**The emotional context of enrollment:**

Families arrive at therapy centers after a long, exhausting diagnostic journey. Research published in Tandfonline (2025) specifically studied the emotional experience of Indian autism care:

"Families quietly withdraw from autism services under the cumulative strain of exhaustion, financial pressures and emotional fatigue, producing what researchers call 'invisible exits.' In a system where inclusion demands consistent attendance, steady fees, fluent interpretation and quiet compliance, many caregivers find themselves falling short of resources." Source: [Tandfonline — Emotional bureaucracies in Indian autism care](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09687599.2025.2558554)

"Caregivers, most often mothers, must repeatedly demonstrate patience, gratitude and compliance to secure support, with class, caste, gender and language shaping these dynamics." Source: [Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09687599.2025.2558554)

**Caregiver burden:**

"Education, socio-economic status and family type are significantly associated with caregiver strain, with caregivers of children with autism having higher strain levels in low education and lower middle socio-economic status households." Source: [NJCM India — Caregiver burden](https://njcmindia.com/index.php/file/article/download/5276/2308)

[ASSUMPTION] Parents who experience a structured, professional intake process — with a clear explanation of assessment results and the initial therapy plan — are more likely to trust the center and remain enrolled. Not validated in the Indian context through primary research.

## Implications for Problem Definition

1. **Intake is a trust-critical moment.** The quality of the enrollment experience shapes long-term retention. A disorganized intake actively risks early dropout.
2. **The gap between assessment and program design is a clinical quality risk.** When assessment results are not systematically transferred to the treating therapist, early sessions are misaligned.
3. **DPDPA 2023 compliance is a real and growing risk.** A product that makes compliance a byproduct of normal intake workflow could be a meaningful differentiator.
4. **UDID documentation support is an underserved but important need.** Centers regularly assist families with UDID applications — producing documentation ad hoc with no structured workflow.
5. **No India-specific intake tool exists** — but demand at Indian price points is unvalidated. Requires primary research.

---

## Gaps — What Secondary Research Cannot Answer (needs primary fieldwork)

1. What does intake actually look like step-by-step at Indian centers? Where do delays concentrate?
2. How much calendar time elapses from first inquiry to first therapy session?
3. What documentation do centers actually collect at intake vs. what they're supposed to collect?
4. What do parents experience emotionally during intake?
5. What is the enrollment drop-off rate — how many families who make initial contact fail to enroll?
6. Do center directors track inquiries and pipeline at all?
7. How aware are center directors of DPDPA 2023 and its applicability to their intake workflows?
8. How is the initial therapy program communicated to parents?
9. What would make a center director willing to change their intake process?
