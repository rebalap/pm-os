## 1. Therapist Onboarding Experience

### What we're discussing
Creating a seamless onboarding journey for therapists — video bytes, explainers, and supporting resources.

### UK context
- **HCPC is the regulatory anchor for UK clinical staff**, not CPD hours. All SLTs (Speech and Language Therapists) and OTs (Occupational Therapists) are registered with the **Health and Care Professions Counci**l. HCPC doesn't mandate minimum hours — it requires a documented *mix* of activities that benefit practice, reviewed every 2 years. This means our onboarding content can be framed as HCPC-eligible CPD evidence, but only if accompanied by structured reflection logs — the video content alone is not enough.
- **Microlearning is the NHS norm.** The NHS e-Learning for Healthcare (e-LfH) platform is the default clinical training infrastructure: 500+ programmes, 16,500+ resources, free, bite-sized (5–15 minutes per unit), mobile-accessible. Any Cognitivebotics onboarding that doesn't fit this pattern will feel unfamiliar to NHS-adjacent therapists.
- **NHS England published Digital Capabilities Frameworks** for each clinical profession. Mapping our onboarding content to these frameworks is a credibility signal with Integrated Care Boards (ICBs) and NHS procurement.
- **DCB0129/DCB0160 digital clinical safety standards** apply to software used alongside NHS services. This doesn't directly block private center adoption but is the trust marker NHS-connected buyers look for.

### Questions for Venkat
1. Are the UK therapy centers we're targeting primarily private/independent or NHS-commissioned? The onboarding design, compliance posture, and CPD angle are materially different depending on the answer.
2. Is there appetite to seek formal HCPC CPD recognition for our therapist modules, and does the current content quality meet the "mix of learning types" threshold HCPC auditors look for?
3. What is the current onboarding completion rate in India? The NHS benchmark average is 68% — knowing where we stand helps us set realistic UK targets.

## 2. Podcasts & Awareness Initiatives

### What we're discussing
Podcast opportunities to drive awareness, with strategic tagging of Cognitivebotics.

### UK context
- **The UK autism podcast landscape is fragmented and parent-/autistic adult-led**, not clinician-led. Key shows: *The Autism Podcast* (London Autism Group Charity, clinical hosts Dr Sophia Christophi + Simeon Thompson), *Let's Talk Autism* (parent-hosted), *Autism Unpacked* (Options Autism), *The Middletown Podcast* (practitioner-facing, autism-affirming). None are dominant.
- **There is no widely recognised UK podcast aimed at SLTs, OTs, or behaviour analysts** working in autism and SEN. This is an open category — a Cognitivebotics-hosted or co-hosted podcast for practitioner-professionals could own uncontested territory.
- **Strategic tagging** means category and keyword metadata, not just show title. Apple Podcasts requires a primary category + up to 2 subcategories — the relevant taxonomy for us is *Health & Fitness > Mental Health*, *Kids & Family > Parenting*, and *Education > How To*. Since March 2024, Apple auto-transcribes English audio, so spoken keywords in episodes are indexed. Correct tagging can improve discovery by 20–25%.
- **Spotify has overtaken Apple as the primary platform for UK health podcast listening** (2024 data). Both platforms require separate optimisation.
- **Guest credibility beats production quality in this niche.** UK autism podcast audiences value autistic voices, parent voices, and NHS clinicians over commercial content. Cognitivebotics content should lead with those voices, not brand messaging.

### Questions for Venkat
1. Is the podcast play being positioned as brand awareness (general public) or therapist acquisition/trust-building (clinical professionals)? The content strategy and guest list differ significantly.
2. Does Venkat have existing relationships with the hosts of *The Autism Podcast* or Options Autism that could enable guest appearances rather than launching a new show?
3. What is Venkat's read on whether UK clinical practitioners consume podcasts professionally, or whether podcasts in this space are primarily a parent-facing channel?

## 3. Screening Tool Integrations

### What we're discussing
Evaluating integrations with third-party screening tools such as ASDetect and similar platforms.

### UK context
- **ASDetect (La Trobe University, Australia)** is a video-based parent-facing mobile app for pre-2.5-year-olds, built on the SACS methodology, with 83% accuracy in identifying early autism signs. There is **no NICE-recommended or NHS-endorsed equivalent app in the UK.** The gap is real.
- **M-CHAT-R/F is the most widely used early screening instrument in UK primary care** — a 2-stage parent-report tool for children aged 16–30 months, used in health visitor checks and GP referral pathways. The Stage 2 follow-up interview is rarely used in practice, limiting its effectiveness. It is form-based, not app-based.
- **Q-CHAT-10 is a validated UK alternative** developed by the Autism Research Centre at Cambridge. Less primary care penetration than M-CHAT but strong research credibility — the Cambridge connection is valuable for partnership positioning.
- **The NHS autism assessment waiting list is the critical pressure point.** As of June 2024: 193,203 open referrals for suspected autism in England; 87.2% waiting more than 13 weeks against NICE's 13-week standard. This systemic backlog makes pre-diagnostic parent-support tools — and tools that support children during the waiting period — politically urgent. This is Cognitivebotics' structural opening in the UK.
- **ADOS-2, SRS-2, BOSA are diagnostic instruments**, not screening tools — they require trained clinical administration and are post-referral. Integration at the screening and monitoring layer (M-CHAT, Q-CHAT) is more defensible than at the diagnostic instrument layer.

### Questions for Venkat
1. Is the integration proposal about ingesting data from an existing screening tool, or building our own screening functionality? These are different investment decisions (partnership vs. build).
2. Has there been any conversation with NHS ICBs about Cognitivebotics being part of the post-referral pathway — families waiting 9+ months for diagnosis could use the platform as structured interim support?
3. Is there a research partnership angle with the Cambridge ARC (Q-CHAT owners) that could lend clinical credibility to a UK screening integration?

## 4. Guest Lectures as a Value-Add

### What we're discussing
Leveraging global expert lectures and videos to create a learning experience for users.

### UK context
- **HCPC CPD is outcomes-focused, not hours-based.** HCPC does not approve or endorse specific CPD activities. A video lecture library qualifies as CPD only if paired with documented reflection logs — the video alone is not auditable evidence. Any CPD offering must have a structured reflection/documentation component to be HCPC-compliant.
- **RCSLT (speech therapists) and RCOT (occupational therapists) have their own CPD portals** and do endorse external training. Third-party providers can apply for listing/endorsement — this is a realistic route for UK CPD positioning and would be a significant credibility signal.
- **The Autism Education Trust (AET) is the benchmark for autism CPD in UK schools and SEN settings.** It is government-funded, co-produced with autistic young people, video-based and on-demand — directly comparable to what Cognitivebotics could offer. Any CPD play will be measured against AET. We either need to be meaningfully differentiated (clinical/therapist focus vs. AET's educator focus) or consider partnership.
- **UK-SBA (UK Society for Behaviour Analysis) is creating a vacancy.** Post-2026, BACB is withdrawing from certifying UK candidates. UK-SBA's Professional Standards Authority accreditation is growing as the domestic alternative. UK-SBA does not currently accredit CPD courses — this is an opening for Cognitivebotics to partner with UK-SBA for ABA practitioner CPD.
- **There is no dominant private video CPD provider for UK autism/SEN therapists.** Private practitioners piece together content from RCSLT, RCOT, conference recordings, and YouTube. A curated library with documented reflection logs would fill a real gap.

### Questions for Venkat
1. Has Venkat identified any existing UK CPD providers Cognitivebotics could partner with or white-label through, rather than building accreditation from scratch?
2. Is the CPD library being positioned as a revenue line, a therapist acquisition/retention mechanism, or a compliance bundle within the main platform subscription?
3. What would it take to seek RCSLT or RCOT endorsement for existing Cognitivebotics training content — and is this in the UK go-to-market plan?

## 5. LM Tool for Query Management

### What we're discussing
Implementing a language model tool to handle and streamline queries.

### UK context
- **ICO designated AI as a 2024/25 enforcement priority.** The ICO's AI and Biometrics Strategy (AIBS, 2024) includes enforcement notices already issued (e.g., against Snap's My AI). An AI ADM (automated decision-making) code of practice is expected. Processing health data of minors via LLMs is **high-risk processing under UK GDPR** and requires a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) before deployment.
- **UK GDPR Article 22 applies to AI outputs with legal or significant effects** on individuals. For query management (FAQ responses, administrative routing), the risk is lower — but any output that could be interpreted as clinical guidance requires human-in-the-loop design and clear disclaimers.
- **The most credible current LLM deployments in UK clinical settings are administrative.** Nuance DAX (ambient clinical documentation) and OpenEvidence (GP clinical query support) are the 2024/25 leaders — both are scribe/summarisation tools. Patient-facing health chatbots remain politically sensitive in the UK following the Babylon Health controversy.
- **Cyber Essentials Plus is the baseline for NHS-adjacent SaaS.** Any tool touching NHS data infrastructure needs external-audited Cyber Essentials certification — not just self-assessment.
- **Strongest defensible UK use cases for an LM query tool:** (a) parent FAQ bot answering non-clinical questions about sessions, content, home practice; (b) therapist admin query routing — scheduling, notes, session summaries; (c) summarisation of session data for therapist review. Clinical recommendation outputs should remain human-in-the-loop.

### Questions for Venkat
1. Has Cognitivebotics obtained UK GDPR legal advice and completed a DPIA for its existing AI features — specifically around processing health data of minors?
2. What is the documented boundary between "informational" and "clinical" in our planned LM outputs? The ICO will ask for this.
3. Does Venkat have visibility on any NHS pilot programmes for AI-supported query management in community paediatric services that Cognitivebotics could align with or reference?

## 6. AWW Training Material & Documentation

### What we're discussing
Developing comprehensive training materials and documents for AWW (Anytime, Anywhere, With Anyone).

### What AWW is
AWW is the practitioner shorthand for **generalisation across three dimensions**: time (Anytime), setting (Anywhere), and person (With Anyone). It is not a branded programme — it is the clinical design principle derived from Stokes and Baer's (1977) generalisation taxonomy, foundational to ABA therapy. The goal is ensuring skills learned in the clinic transfer to the home, school, and community — with parents, teachers, and peers, not just the therapist.

### UK context
- **AWW generalisation is an ABA-rooted term.** UK clinical audiences — particularly SLTs and OTs — are more familiar with "generalisation" as a standalone term. The AWW label may need translation for non-ABA practitioners. The concept is universal; the label is US/ABA-specific.
- **Parent training is the primary mechanism for AWW.** Research consistently shows that including parents in therapy delivery is the most effective route to achieving AWW generalisation. WHO's Caregiver Skills Training (CST) — now available as a virtual platform (eCST) — is the most widely cited evidence-based parent training framework for this. Any Cognitivebotics AWW documentation should reference or complement this framework.
- **There is no formalised documentation standard for AWW progress.** Neither NHS, RCSLT, RCOT, nor BACB mandate a specific format. Good AWW documentation in a digital platform captures: (a) which settings a target behaviour has been observed; (b) which people have prompted or observed it; (c) prompted vs. independent response rates across settings and people; (d) parent-reported home session data. Cognitivebotics' existing prompted/independent/attempts data model maps directly onto this — this is an existing product strength that needs surfacing.
- **The Cognitivebotics core proposition IS the AWW argument.** The platform bridges clinic and home — exactly what AWW requires. Clinic-only tools cannot evidence AWW. Cognitivebotics can. This is a differentiator that should be made explicit in UK clinical marketing and in training materials.
- **Cognitivebotics' JMIR study is the only published evidence we have.** The 12-month study showed significant improvements in CARS, Vineland Social Age, and language. If any of those gains were measured at home (not just clinic), that is the AWW evidence story. We need to confirm whether the study design captures cross-setting generalisation.

### Questions for Venkat
1. Does the platform currently generate cross-setting comparison reports — can a therapist see prompted vs. independent response rates at clinic vs. at home side by side? If not, this is the product gap for AWW.
2. Is AWW/generalisation language used in Cognitivebotics' current therapist-facing UI and sales materials, or is it implicit? UK clinical buyers will respond to explicit framing.
3. Is there a case study — from the JMIR study or current user base — that demonstrates measurable AWW outcomes: a skill trained in clinic and observed independently at home? This is the UK proof point we need.
